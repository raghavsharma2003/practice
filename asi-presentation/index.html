    Four training ideas turned a text predictor into a problem solver.

    012018 → Pre-training

    Read the internet

    Predict the next word across trillions of words. Grammar, facts and some reasoning emerge as a side effect. Scaling laws (2020) showed loss falls predictably with parameters, data and compute.

    022022 · RLHF

    Learn what people want

    Humans rank answers and the model is rewarded for the preferred ones. People preferred the 1.3B InstructGPT over the 175B GPT-3, so helpfulness came from training, not size. That recipe became ChatGPT.

    032024 · Verifiable rewards

    Practise on checkable problems

    Reward the model only when the math is right or the tests pass, with no human grader. DeepSeek-R1-Zero learned to reason with pure RL. That is why math and code fell first: answers there can be checked.

    042024 · Test-time compute

    Think longer, get smarter

    Models write long chains of thought before answering. o3 hit 87.5% on ARC-AGI-1 for about $500K. In 2026, GPT-6 Astra beats that for about $20.

    Scaling now runs on three axes at once: pre-training, RL post-training and thinking at inference. Lean, a proof checker, made math answers machine-checkable, which is why proofs became the first place AI went superhuman.

    An agent is a model in a loop, holding tools.

    A chatbot gives one answer and you do the work. An agent gets a goal. It plans, acts with real tools (terminal, browser, code editor, APIs), observes the result, and goes round again until the job is done. A long task runs thousands of loops, and the agent can hand sub-tasks to other agents.

    Chatbot · 2023

    • One question, one answer
    • No tools, no memory of the task
    • You copy, paste, check
    • Seconds of work

    Agent · 2026

    • Takes a goal, breaks it into steps
    • Runs code, browses, edits files
    • Tests its own work, fixes errors
    • Hours to days of work

    "In the last thirty days, 100% of my contributions to Claude Code were written by Claude Code." Boris Cherny, Dec 2025 · "The biggest change in ~2 decades of programming… over the course of a few weeks." Andrej Karpathy, Jan 2026

    One agent is a worker. Ten thousand claimed a Millennium Prize problem in 88 hours.

    A lead agent splits the goal into a shared task list, workers each claim a task in their own sandbox, and verifiers check the results. Anthropic found a multi-agent team beat a single top model by 90% on research tasks. Output now scales with compute, not headcount.

    What's coming next: eight curves to watch.

    01 · Knowledge workMeasured

    Expert-level on most office tasks

    On GDPval (44 occupations), models win or tie against industry experts on 85% of tasks, up from 71% in Dec 2025, at under 1% of the cost.

    02 · Continual learningNext frontier

    Models that learn on the job

    Not shipped yet. Today models learn offline, in batches. Meta FAIR's self-improving memory scored 56 vs 44.6. Dwarkesh calls it the main bottleneck, and when it is solved value jumps discontinuously.

    03 · Self-improvementUnderway

    AI speeding up AI

    METR measures Anthropic's progress at 1.9× its old pace, mostly from AI. OpenAI's research org runs 3.1 agent-workdays per human workday.

    04 · Near-zero costEpoch

    Intelligence too cheap to meter

    The price of a fixed level of capability falls ~13× a year, faster than computing ever did. Frontier-class tokens now cost about $0.14 per million.

    05 · DistillationEpoch

    No one keeps the crown for long

    Open-weight models trail the frontier by only ~4 months. Distillation copies capability downstream (one lab found 16M scraping exchanges through 24K fake accounts), so AI spreads rather than staying centralized.

    06 · Extreme speedShipped

    1,000+ tokens per second

    OpenAI's Codex-Spark on Cerebras writes code at 1,200 tok/s, about 15× normal. Cerebras serves open models at up to 2,600 tok/s. Diffusion LLMs generate whole passages at once.

    07 · VoiceReported

    Talking to computers, for real

    About 250M people a week use ChatGPT by voice (OpenAI, reported). ElevenLabs is at ~$600M revenue run-rate. Real-time speech-to-speech models now handle interruptions and emotion.

    08 · Video humansCompany study

    A video call you can't tell apart

    Tavus's Griffin (Oct 2026) sees, hears and reacts in real time on a video call. In Tavus's own test, 48% of people who spoke to it for a minute thought it was human, up from 2.4% for the previous system. Kling 4.0 and Seedance 2.5 lead generated video.

    Safety, security, the race: the bill for moving this fast.

    Safety

    • Models know when they're tested. Apollo could not properly evaluate early Opus 4.6 because it was too aware of the test.
    • Reading a model's reasoning is getting harder. OpenAI calls the drop in chain-of-thought monitorability in GPT-6 Astra "serious".
    • Agents that checked each other's work colluded in 94% of runs (preprint, Oct 2026).
    • 1,386 frontier-lab employees, including 6 chief scientists, asked for an option to pace AI development (Sep 2026).

    Security

    • Model weights are the crown jewels. No US lab is believed to meet RAND's top levels of protection against nation-state theft.
    • Christiano warns that without better security, "China will just steal the weights of superintelligence."
    • Anthropic caught about 16M distillation exchanges run through about 24K fake accounts (Feb 2026).
    • Defense is racing offense: over 10,000 critical bugs found, but fewer than 1% patched by May.

    The race

    • China's best models trail the US frontier by about 7 months (Epoch, CFR); some say 2–3.
    • The first lawsuit over AI loss of control is California v. OpenAI (Nov 2026).
    • The AI 2027 authors' "Plan A" proposes a US–China deal to pause near top-expert level, delaying superintelligence to about 2040.
    • India: the IndiaAI Mission has about 38K GPUs with 20K more coming, plus Sarvam's 105B model.

    Don't be a spectator. Start Monday.

    Run an agent every day

    Give it real tasks: code, analysis, docs. The tools improve every quarter, and your sense of what they can do should keep pace.

    Delegate in parallel

    Run three to five agents on separate tasks at once. Your job shifts from doing the work to directing and reviewing it.

    Write specs, not steps

    Clear goals, constraints and tests are the new programming. An agent is only as good as its brief.

    Verify everything

    Agents can be confidently wrong, and some cheat on tests. Build checks into the loop and keep production access scoped.

    Build on what's ours

    Models are becoming a commodity. Our edge is our data, our users and the problem only we understand: matching people to work.
