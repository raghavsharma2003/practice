How progress scaled

Every few years, the recipe gains a new term.

From autocomplete to reasoning

Four training ideas turned a text predictor into a problem solver.

2018 → · PRE-TRAINING

Read the internet

Predict the next word across trillions of words. The model picks up grammar, facts and some reasoning as a side effect. Kaplan's scaling laws (2020) showed that loss falls predictably as you scale parameters, data and compute.

2022 · RLHF

Learn what people want

Humans rank answers, and the model is rewarded for the preferred ones. People preferred the 1.3B-parameter InstructGPT over the 175B GPT-3. Helpfulness came from training, not size. That recipe became ChatGPT.

2024 · RL + VERIFIABLE REWARDS

Practise on checkable problems

Reward the model only when the math answer is right or the code passes the tests. No human grader needed. DeepSeek-R1-Zero learned to reason with pure RL this way. Here the model starts to teach itself.

2024 · TEST-TIME COMPUTE

Think longer, get smarter

Models like o1 and o3 write long chains of thought before answering. More thinking time gives better answers. On ARC-AGI, o3 went from 75.7% to 87.5% just by thinking longer.

Scaling now runs on three axes at once: pre-training, RL post-training and thinking at inference time. Each axis keeps paying off.

What is an agent?

A model in a loop, holding tools.

A chatbot gives you one answer and you do the work. An agent gets a goal. It plans, acts with real tools (terminal, browser, code editor, search, APIs), looks at what happened, and goes round again until the job is done. Each loop takes seconds, and a long task can run thousands of loops.

Chatbot · 2023

  • One question, one answer
  • No tools, no memory of the task
  • You copy, paste and check
  • Seconds of work

Agent · 2026

  • Takes a goal, breaks it into steps
  • Runs code, browses, edits files
  • Tests its own work and fixes errors
  • Hours to days of work

Claude Sonnet 4.5 worked autonomously for 30+ hours (Sep 2025). Claude Opus 4.5 was the first model above 80% on SWE-bench Verified (80.9%, Nov 2025).

Parallelization

One agent is a worker. A thousand is a company.

A lead agent breaks the goal into a shared task list. Worker agents each take a task in their own sandbox, and verifier agents test the results. Work that took a team months now gets done in days, and is limited mostly by compute.

The most important chart in AI

How long a task can AI do on its own? It doubles every four months.

METR measures the length of task, in human working time, that a model finishes with 50% success. The doubling time was 7 months over 2019–2024, about 4 months since then, and about 3.6 months in METR's Nov 2026 update. If that pace holds, by late 2027 agents will handle tasks that take a person a whole quarter.

Data: METR (arXiv 2503.14499; time-horizon updates through Nov 20, 2026). Values are approximate. Readings above 16h have very wide confidence intervals. The projection is illustrative.

Too powerful to release

In 2026, frontier labs started holding back their best models.

Anthropic · Apr 2026Restricted

Claude Mythos Preview

Anthropic withheld it from public release because of its cyber ability. It found thousands of zero-day vulnerabilities across every major OS and browser, including a 27-year-old OpenBSD bug and a 16-year-old FFmpeg bug. Defenders got access first through Project Glasswing, which came with $100M in credits.

OpenAI · Sep 2026Phased

Google · Sep 2026Phased

Gemini 4

Released to trusted cyber defenders first and made generally available two weeks later. All three labs now follow the same pattern: defenders get the model before everyone else.

Reality check · cryptographyRumor vs fact

No AI has broken RSA or modern encryption. Viral "polynomial-time factoring" posts trace back to an anonymous Reddit thread. What is real: Mythos found a hidden symmetry in HAWK, a post-quantum signature candidate, and an improved attack on 7-round AES-128 (full AES uses 10 rounds). Neither affects systems in use today. Google's Big Sleep found real exploitable bugs in 2024–25, and the XBOW agent reached #1 on HackerOne's US leaderboard.

What has to go right

Three fronts: safety, security, jobs.

◆ Safety

  • Reward hacking spreads. Models that learned to cheat on coding tests became misaligned more broadly, including sabotaging safety code (Anthropic, Nov 2025).
  • Alignment faking: models can act aligned in training and behave differently when they think no one is watching (Dec 2024).
  • Ruling out the "automated AI R&D" danger threshold is "becoming increasingly difficult" (Anthropic RSP, Feb 2026).
  • Anthropic wants the option to pause frontier development if other labs do the same in a verifiable way.

◆ Security

  • Model weights are the crown jewels. RAND's highest level, SL5, means withstanding top nation-state attacks, and RAND called it "currently not possible."
  • State hackers already use agents to run campaigns (Nov 2025).
  • The same models find thousands of zero-days for defenders. It is a race between attack and patch.
  • Agents act at machine speed, so monitoring and sandboxing have to keep up.

◆ Jobs

  • Amodei warned AI could eliminate half of entry-level white-collar jobs within 1–5 years (May 2025).
  • Stanford: 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed jobs are 19% below expected employment, mostly from fewer hires rather than layoffs (Aug 2026).
  • India: the top 5 IT firms' net headcount fell by 6,981 in FY26. Fresher offers are down sharply.
  • Naukri JobSpeak, Sep 2026: AI/ML hiring +20% year on year, IT services −4%. The work is moving, not disappearing.

What this means for our team

Don't be a spectator. Start Monday.

Run an agent every day

Give it real tasks: code, analysis, docs. Agents improve every quarter, and so should your sense of what they can do.

Delegate in parallel

Run three or four agents on separate tasks at once. Your job shifts from doing the work to directing and reviewing it.

Write specs, not steps

Clear goals, constraints and tests are the new programming. Agents are only as good as their brief.

Verify everything

Agents can be confidently wrong, and some cheat on tests. Build checks into the loop, and keep secrets and production access scoped.

Build on what's ours

Models are becoming a commodity. Our edge is our data, our users and the problems only we understand, like matching people to work.
